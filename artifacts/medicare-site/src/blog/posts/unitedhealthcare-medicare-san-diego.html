AARP Medicare Plans — What the Brand Means

UnitedHealthcare markets many of its Medicare plans under the AARP name through a licensing agreement. The AARP name carries brand recognition and trust, but it's important to understand that AARP doesn't underwrite or administer the plans — UnitedHealthcare does. The plans are UHC plans with AARP branding. You do not need to be an AARP member to enroll in most AARP-branded Medicare plans.

Renew Active Fitness Benefit

UHC's Renew Active program provides gym membership access through a broad network of fitness facilities — including many San Diego-area locations. UnitedHealthcare Part D Plans

UHC is also one of the largest Part D plan sponsors in the country. Their standalone prescription drug plans are available in San Diego for beneficiaries on Original Medicare. UHC Part D plans have broad formularies, but tier placement and cost-sharing vary — comparing your specific medications is essential before choosing a plan.

AARP Medicare Supplement (Medigap) Plans

UHC sells AARP-branded Medigap plans in California. These plans pay the gaps Original Medicare leaves — deductibles, copays, coinsurance — giving you predictable costs. AARP/UHC Medigap premiums are competitive, and because Medigap benefits are standardized by law, comparing UHC's rates against other carriers is a standard part of the review process.
