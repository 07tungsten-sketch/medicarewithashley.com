When Does My Medicare Coverage Actually Start?

Your Medicare eligibility begins on the first day of the month you turn 65. But your enrollment window — called the Initial Enrollment Period (IEP) — is actually a 7-month window:

  • 3 months before your birthday month
  • Your birthday month
  • 3 months after your birthday month

The earlier you enroll within this window, the sooner your coverage starts. If you wait until the last 3 months, your coverage may be delayed by 1–3 months.

What Are the Medicare Late Enrollment Penalties?

Part B penalty: Your Part B premium increases by 10% for every 12-month period you were eligible but didn't enroll — and this penalty is permanent. On a $185/month premium, that adds up fast over a 20-year retirement.

Part D penalty: 1% of the national base premium for every month you went without creditable drug coverage. Also permanent.

The good news: if you take the right steps at the right time, these penalties are completely avoidable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to sign up for Medicare if I have coverage through my spouse's employer?

It depends on the size of your spouse's employer. If their company has 20 or more employees, you can usually delay Medicare without penalty. Smaller employers may make Medicare primary, meaning you should enroll on time.
