<h1>Medicare Advantage vs. Medicare Supplement: Which Is Right for You in San Diego?</h1>

<p>One of the most common questions Ashley Watson hears from San Diego residents approaching Medicare is: <em>"Should I get a Medicare Advantage plan or a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan?"</em></p>

<p>The honest answer: it depends on your health, your budget, and how you prefer to use your coverage. Here's a clear, side-by-side breakdown to help you decide — with no sales pressure attached.</p>

<h2>What Is Medicare Advantage?</h2>

<p>Medicare Advantage (Part C) is an all-in-one plan offered by private insurance companies that replaces your Original Medicare. Most plans bundle Part A (hospital), Part B (medical), and Part D (prescriptions) into one card — often with a $0 monthly premium. Many also include extras like dental, vision, and hearing coverage.</p>

<p>The trade-off: Medicare Advantage plans use provider networks. You typically need to see doctors within the plan's network, and you'll pay copays and coinsurance each time you use care. Out-of-pocket costs can vary significantly depending on how much healthcare you use in a given year.</p>

<h2>What Is a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) Plan?</h2>

<p>A Medicare Supplement plan works alongside Original Medicare. You keep Medicare as your primary insurance, and the Supplement plan covers the gaps — copays, coinsurance, and deductibles — that Medicare doesn't pay.</p>

<p>The big advantage: you can see <strong>any doctor or hospital in the country that accepts Medicare</strong>, with no network restrictions. Your costs are highly predictable, often with little to no out-of-pocket spending once you've met your deductible. The trade-off is a monthly premium, which can range from $80 to $200+ depending on the plan letter and your age.</p>

<h2>Which Is Better for San Diego Residents?</h2>

<p>San Diego has excellent Medicare Advantage plan competition, which means more $0-premium options and strong extra benefits compared to many other parts of the country. If you're generally healthy, on a fixed income, and prefer to keep monthly costs low, Medicare Advantage can be a great fit.</p>

<p>On the other hand, if you have ongoing health conditions, see specialists frequently, or travel often outside California, a Medicare Supplement plan gives you the flexibility and predictability that Advantage plans can't always match.</p>

<p>There's no universal right answer — which is exactly why working with an independent Medicare broker in San Diego matters. Ashley can look at your specific health history, your doctors, your medications, and your budget to give you a genuine recommendation.</p>

<h2>When Can You Switch?</h2>

<p>You can switch between Medicare Advantage plans during the Annual Enrollment Period (October 15 – December 7) each year. Switching <em>to</em> a Medicare Supplement plan after your initial enrollment can be more complicated — you may need to pass medical underwriting in most states. California has some additional protections, but timing still matters. The earlier you enroll in a Supplement plan, the more options you typically have.</p>
