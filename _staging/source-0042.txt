<h1>Can You Have Medicare and Medi-Cal? Dual Coverage in California Explained</h1>

<p>Yes — and if you qualify, it's one of the most powerful combinations available in California healthcare. People who are eligible for both Medicare and Medi-Cal are called <strong>"dual eligible,"</strong> and the benefits can be substantial: reduced or eliminated premiums, copays, and prescription costs, and in some cases, additional benefits not available through Medicare alone.</p>

<h2>What Is Dual Eligibility?</h2>

<p>Dual eligibility simply means you qualify for both programs at the same time:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Medicare</strong> — federal health insurance, primarily for people 65 and older or those with certain disabilities</li>
  <li><strong>Medi-Cal</strong> — California's Medicaid program, which provides health coverage based on income and assets</li>
</ul>

<p>When you're dual eligible, Medi-Cal acts as a "secondary payer" — it fills in the gaps that Medicare leaves, like copays, deductibles, and premiums. For many seniors, this combination results in little to no out-of-pocket healthcare costs.</p>

<h2>Who Qualifies for Both Medicare and Medi-Cal?</h2>

<p>To qualify for Medi-Cal alongside Medicare, you generally need to meet income and asset limits set by the state of California. The limits change periodically, but as a general guide:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Individuals with monthly income at or below approximately 138% of the Federal Poverty Level may qualify for full Medi-Cal</li>
  <li>Those with slightly higher incomes may qualify for a Medicare Savings Program (see below)</li>
  <li>California has relatively generous eligibility thresholds compared to many other states</li>
</ul>

<p>Many seniors don't realize they may qualify — especially those on a fixed income, receiving SSI, or with limited assets. It's always worth checking.</p>

<h2>What Are Medicare Savings Programs?</h2>

<p>Even if you don't qualify for full Medi-Cal benefits, you may qualify for a <strong>Medicare Savings Program (MSP)</strong>. These are state programs that help pay some or all of your Medicare costs:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Qualified Medicare Beneficiary (QMB):</strong> Pays your Part A and Part B premiums, deductibles, and copays</li>
  <li><strong>Specified Low-Income Medicare Beneficiary (SLMB):</strong> Pays your Part B premium only</li>
  <li><strong>Qualifying Individual (QI):</strong> Pays most of your Part B premium</li>
</ul>

<p>Qualifying for QMB also automatically qualifies you for Extra Help — the federal subsidy that significantly reduces Part D prescription drug costs.</p>

<h2>What Is a Dual Special Needs Plan (D-SNP)?</h2>

<p>If you're dual eligible, you may be able to enroll in a <strong>Dual Special Needs Plan (D-SNP)</strong>. These are a type of Medicare Advantage plan specifically designed for people with both Medicare and Medi-Cal. D-SNPs often include:</p>

<ul>
  <li>$0 premiums</li>
  <li>Coordinated care between your Medicare and Medi-Cal benefits</li>
  <li>Additional benefits like transportation to medical appointments, meal delivery, and over-the-counter allowances</li>
  <li>Dedicated care coordination teams</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Will having Medi-Cal affect my Medicare benefits?</h3>
<p>No. Having Medi-Cal does not reduce your Medicare benefits. It simply adds a layer of coverage that pays costs Medicare doesn't.</p>

<h3>Do I have to choose between Medicare and Medi-Cal?</h3>
<p>No. You can have both at the same time. That's the whole point of dual eligibility.</p>

<h3>How do I apply for Medi-Cal in San Diego?</h3>
<p>You can apply through the San Diego County Health and Human Services Agency, by mail, or online through CoveredCA.com. Ashley can help you prepare your application and understand what documentation you'll need.</p>
