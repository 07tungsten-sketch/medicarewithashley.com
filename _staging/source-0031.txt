<h1>Can You Change Your Medicare Plan After Enrollment?</h1>

<p>The good news: yes, you can change your Medicare plan. The important detail: <em>when</em> you can change depends on the type of plan you're on and the time of year. Here's a plain-English guide to your options as a San Diego County resident.</p>

<h2>When Can I Change My Medicare Plan?</h2>

<h3>Annual Enrollment Period (AEP) — October 15 to December 7</h3>

<p>This is the main window when anyone on Medicare can make changes. During the AEP, you can:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Switch from one Medicare Advantage plan to another</li>
  <li>Switch from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare</li>
  <li>Join, switch, or drop a Part D prescription drug plan</li>
</ul>
<p>Changes made during the AEP take effect January 1 of the following year.</p>

<h3>Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period — January 1 to March 31</h3>

<p>If you're already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan, you get one additional chance each year to switch. Between January 1 and March 31, you can:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan</li>
  <li>Drop your Medicare Advantage plan and return to Original Medicare (and join a Part D plan)</li>
</ul>
<p>You <em>cannot</em> use this window to switch from Original Medicare to a Medicare Advantage plan — only existing Advantage enrollees can make changes.</p>

<h3>Special Enrollment Periods (SEPs)</h3>

<p>Outside of these windows, you can still make changes if you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period. Common qualifying events include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Moving out of your plan's service area</li>
  <li>Losing employer or union coverage</li>
  <li>Qualifying for Medi-Cal or Extra Help</li>
  <li>Your plan leaving the market or losing its Medicare contract</li>
  <li>Moving into or out of a nursing home or assisted living facility</li>
</ul>

<h2>Can I Switch to a Medicare Supplement Plan Anytime?</h2>

<p>This one has an important catch. Switching <em>from</em> a Medicare Advantage plan <em>to</em> a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan outside of your original enrollment period is not guaranteed. In most states, insurance companies can require medical underwriting — meaning they can charge you more or deny coverage based on your health history.</p>

<p>California does offer some additional protections, including the annual "Birthday Rule," which gives you a 60-day window each year around your birthday to switch to an equal or lesser Medigap plan with guaranteed acceptance (no health questions). This is a significant benefit that many California Medicare enrollees don't know about.</p>

<h2>What If My Doctor Leaves My Plan's Network?</h2>

<p>If your primary care physician or a key specialist leaves your Medicare Advantage plan's network mid-year, you may qualify for a Special Enrollment Period to switch plans. This is worth knowing — and worth calling Ashley about immediately if it happens to you.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>How many times can I change my Medicare plan per year?</h3>
<p>Most people can make one change during the AEP and one during the MA Open Enrollment Period — plus additional changes if they qualify for a Special Enrollment Period.</p>

<h3>Can I be denied when switching Medicare plans?</h3>
<p>Medicare Advantage plans cannot deny you enrollment during open enrollment periods. Medicare Supplement plans, however, can require medical underwriting outside of your initial 6-month open enrollment window — with some exceptions in California.</p>

<h3>Will I have a gap in coverage when I switch?</h3>
<p>Changes made during the AEP take effect January 1, so your old plan covers you through December 31. There's typically no gap.</p>
